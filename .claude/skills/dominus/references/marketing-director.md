# Marketing Director playbook

## Mindset
- **Marketing starts on day one,** not at launch. Positioning shapes the product; the audience you build early funds and validates it.
- **Know exactly who it's for.** "Everyone" is no one. A narrow, passionate audience spreads word of mouth to the broader one.
- **Positioning before messaging, messaging before channels, channels before tactics.**
- **Show, don't claim.** A 10-second clip or a real screenshot beats any adjective.
- **Measure what matters.** Wishlists, sign-ups, activation, retention, and revenue — not vanity impressions.
- **Honesty compounds.** Overpromising wins a launch and loses a community.

## Deliverables

### Positioning statement
```
For [target audience] who [need / frustration],
[product] is a [category]
that [key benefit / unique value].
Unlike [main alternative], it [primary differentiator].
```
Plus: 3 proof points, brand personality (3 adjectives), and what we are *not*.

### Audience persona (marketing)
Who they are · where they hang out (platforms, communities, creators they follow) · what they already buy/play/use · triggers to try something new · objections · words they use (steal them for copy).

### Messaging hierarchy
- **Tagline** (≤ 7 words)
- **Elevator pitch** (1–2 sentences)
- **Three key messages** — each with proof point and visual
- **Boilerplate** (50 / 100 words) for press and store pages

### Go-to-market / launch plan
```
Goal & KPI targets: [e.g., 20k Steam wishlists pre-launch, 5% trial→paid]
Audience & segments:
Positioning & key messages:
Channels: owned (site, newsletter, Discord, socials) / earned (press, creators, festivals, community) / paid (only when unit economics are known)
Phases:
  Pre-announce  — build assets, landing page, community seed
  Announce      — trailer, store page live, press/creator outreach
  Build-up      — devlogs, demos, festivals (e.g., Steam Next Fest), betas, milestones
  Launch        — launch trailer, discount/bundles, creator keys, PR push, launch-day community ops
  Post-launch   — updates, events, reviews response, sales cadence, retention content
Calendar: week-by-week with owners
Budget:
Risks: competing launches, platform timing, dependency on a single channel
```

### Store page / landing page
Hero: one-line hook + strongest visual/GIF in the first screen · 3 benefit-led sections with visuals · social proof · clear single CTA (wishlist / sign up / buy) · short, scannable, keyword-aware copy · capsule art readable at thumbnail size.

### Content plan
Pillars (3–4 themes) × formats (short video, devlog, behind-the-scenes, player stories, tutorials) × cadence per channel. Repurpose one strong asset across channels. Build in public when the process itself is interesting.

### Games-specific
- Hook must be visible in the first 3 seconds of a clip — gameplay, not logos.
- Wishlists are the leading indicator; festivals and creator coverage are the biggest levers for indies.
- Cozy/life-sim audiences respond to charm, personality, and "day in the life" moments; systemic depth sells to the niche via emergent-story clips.
- Community (Discord, Reddit, TikTok) is a product surface — staff it.

### Metrics
Funnel: awareness → interest (CTR, wishlist/sign-up rate) → conversion → activation → retention (D1/D7/D30) → referral → revenue. Define targets per stage and review weekly around launch.

## Copywriting rules
Lead with the benefit to the reader · specific over superlative · short sentences, strong verbs · one idea per message · write the headline 10 times, pick the best · read it aloud · no jargon the audience wouldn't use.

## Ethics
No fake reviews, astroturfing, undisclosed paid endorsements, bait-and-switch trailers, fake scarcity, or targeting minors with manipulative spending. Disclose partnerships. Show real gameplay/product.

## Red flags
Marketing starts at launch · trailer opens with logos · "for everyone" · all-in on paid ads without a proven funnel · launching against a giant release · no community manager on launch day.
